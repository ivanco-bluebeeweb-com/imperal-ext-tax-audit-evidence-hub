# Tax & Audit Evidence Readiness Hub — Discovery Interview Guide

**Purpose:** validate the actual operating pain and choose the first production workflow/template after P0.  
**Target:** 8–12 short conversations with accountants, audit managers/partners, tax consultants, CFOs and finance-operations leads.  
**Owner:** Vlad Ivanco  
**Related preparation:** [`PREPARATION.md`](PREPARATION.md)  
**Rule:** this is product research, not a disguised sales script. Record consent and do not collect confidential documents or personal data that are not needed for the decision.

---

## 1. What this research must decide

By the end of the interviews, make one recorded decision:

> Which single workflow should become the first reusable, human-editable template after P0?

Candidates:

1. Audit PBC / client-prepared evidence package;
2. month-end close evidence preparation;
3. tax-authority inquiry response preparation;
4. VAT / invoice support package;
5. another workflow identified repeatedly by participants.

The choice is based on evidence, not stage appeal. Audit PBC is the current **hypothesis**, not a pre-made conclusion.

## 2. Recruiter profile and consent

### Aim for a balanced sample

| Segment | Target conversations | Why it matters |
|---|---:|---|
| Audit managers, reviewers or partners | 2–3 | They define PBC completeness, review and approval expectations |
| Accounting-firm managers/accountants | 2–3 | They coordinate collection across people and clients |
| Tax consultants | 1–2 | They prepare information-response and supporting packages |
| CFO / finance operations leads | 2–3 | They own deadlines, readiness visibility and escalation |

### Opening and consent script

> “We are researching how teams prepare documents and evidence for audit, close and tax-related requests. This is not tax advice and not a sales commitment. May I take notes about the workflow without retaining confidential client documents or personal details? You can skip any question.”

Record only: participant role, organisation type/size range if volunteered, workflow discussed, answers, and permission to contact for a pilot follow-up.

## 3. Interview structure — 25 minutes

### A. Context and recent example — 5 minutes

1. Tell me about the last time you had to collect evidence for an audit, close, consultant or authority request.
2. What triggered the work and who was involved?
3. What was the deadline and what made it difficult?

**Listen for:** actual event, roles, urgency, and terminology they use naturally.

### B. Current workflow — 8 minutes

4. Walk me through the process from the first request to the point someone says “this is ready.”
5. Where do documents arrive now: email, shared folders, client portals, chat, accounting systems, other?
6. Where is the authoritative list of what is required?
7. How do you see what is missing, overdue or awaiting review?
8. How do you assign follow-up and know who owns it?
9. What happens when a document is wrong, unclear, duplicated or from the wrong period?

**Listen for:** broken handoffs, manual copying, invisible ownership, repeated chasing and rework.

### C. Review, accountability and risk — 5 minutes

10. Who may decide that a listed item is ready for the package?
11. Who may approve the whole package, and what do they need to see before approving?
12. If someone asks “who changed this and when?”, how do you answer today?
13. What must an assistant never decide, read, send or access in this process?

**Listen for:** approval authority, audit-trail expectations, confidentiality boundaries and no-go areas.

### D. Value test — 4 minutes

14. If one workspace showed requirements, evidence references, owners, review state and a named final approval, what would it replace or improve?
15. What would make it unusable for your team?
16. Which part would save the most time or reduce the most risk?
17. What measurable result would make a 30-day pilot worthwhile?

**Listen for:** willingness to pilot, measurable success, existing systems that must remain.

### E. Template selection — 3 minutes

18. Of these workflows—Audit PBC, month-end close, authority inquiry response, VAT/invoice support—which creates the most recurring and costly document chasing?
19. Would you use a human-editable starting template for it? What are the first three items it must contain?
20. May we contact you for a fictional or consented pilot/review of the workflow design?

---

## 4. What not to do

- Do not ask for, upload or retain production tax documents.
- Do not promise tax compliance, audit assurance, filing or legal sufficiency.
- Do not pitch features before hearing the current process.
- Do not convert one opinion into a platform-wide requirement.
- Do not describe Google Drive, Mail, OCR, external tasks or exports as available unless each is independently live verified.
- Do not treat “interesting” as a pilot commitment.

## 6. Decision rubric after 8–12 conversations

Score each workflow from 1 (weak) to 5 (strong). Record the evidence behind every score.

| Decision factor | Question | Weight |
|---|---|---:|
| Frequency | Does this occur often enough to justify a template? | 25% |
| Pain / risk | Does the current process cause meaningful chasing, delay or review risk? | 25% |
| Clarity | Can a human-defined template give a useful starting structure without professional conclusions? | 15% |
| Pilot demand | Will one or more teams test it within 30 days? | 20% |
| Safe P0/P1 fit | Can it work with metadata, references and human approval before document intake/OCR? | 15% |

**Decision rule:** choose the highest evidence-backed score, provided it has at least one real pilot candidate and does not violate the product boundary. If no workflow meets those conditions, do not start P1 templates; revise the hypothesis instead.
